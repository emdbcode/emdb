Trivia

  • "The Word" has been cited as the first Beatles song to approach love in more abstract terms, rather than the straightforward boy-meets-girl themes of songs like “She Loves You”.
  • The song’s message was echoed by many artists throughout the remainder of the 1960s, particularly during 1967’s Summer of Love, when the Beatles amplified it by performing “All You Need Is Love” live for a global television audience on Our World. The same message lies at the heart of Yellow Submarine, in which love triumphs over the Blue Meanies.
  • The song’s lyrics were handwritten by Paul, with illustrations added by him and John using watercolour paints and felt-tip pens (featured below in the Lyrics section). In 1966, Yoko Ono asked John to donate a lyric sheet as a 50th-birthday gift for avant-garde composer John Cage. Lennon gave her the decorated sheet for “The Word”, which became part of the John Cage Notations Project Collection, a historical archive of mid-20th-century music manuscripts, sketches and correspondence.

Words from John and Paul

“
‘The Word’ was written together but it’s mainly mine. You read the words — it’s all about getting smart. It’s the marijuana period. It’s love. It’s a love and peace thing. The word is ‘love’, right?
— John Lennon, The Playboy Interviews, 1981
“
We smoked a bit of pot, then we wrote out a multicoloured lyric sheet, the first time we’d ever done that. We normally didn’t smoke when we were working. It got in the way of songwriting because it would just cloud your mind up — ‘Oh, shit, what are we doing?’ It’s better to be straight. But we did this multicoloured thing.
— Paul McCartney, Many Years from Now, 1997
