Trivia

• “Norwegian Wood (This Bird Has Flown)” is the first example of a rock band using the sitar in one of their songs, played by George Harrison.

• Harrison first encountered a sitar while filming the Indian restaurant scene in Help!, where it was played by one of the background musicians, sparking a lifelong interest in Indian classical music and raga rock.

• Harrison went on to expand his use of the sitar on Beatles tracks such as “Love You To”, “Tomorrow Never Knows”, “Within You Without You” and “The Inner Light”.vv

• John Lennon was the song’s primary writer, completing the lyrics and middle eight with Paul McCartney.

• Bob Dylan parodied “Norwegian Wood” with his 1965 song “4th Time Around.” The song is often interpreted as Dylan’s response to the Beatles’ track, which he reportedly reacted to by saying, “What is this? It’s me, Bob. He’s doing me!”<br><br>
Prior to his song’s release, Dylan played “4th Time Around” on tape to the Beatles at their May Fair suite. Weeks later, an exchange between Dylan and Lennon was filmed in which Dylan confronted Lennon over a remark he had made to Paul McCartney upon hearing the song: that it ought to be published by Northern Songs, the Beatles’ publishing company.<br><br>
When asked about Dylan’s track in an 1968 <em>Rolling Stone</em> interview, Lennon said the song made him feel paranoid and that he told Dylan he didn’t like it.

• In 1967, “Norwegian Wood” was released as an eponymous EP in Australia, alongside three other tracks from <em>Rubber Soul</em>.

Words from John, Paul, and George

"Norwegian Wood" is my song completely. It was about an affair I was having. I was very careful and paranoid because I didn’t want my wife, Cyn, to know that there really was something going on outside of the household. I’d always had some kind of affairs going, so I was trying to be sophisticated in writing about an affair, but in such a smoke-screen way that you couldn’t tell. But I can’t remember any specific woman it had to do with.

— John Lennon (1980), <em>Playboy</em>, 1981

"I came in and he had this first stanza, which was brilliant: "I once had a girl, or should I say, she once had me." That was all he had, no title, no nothing. I said, "Oh yes, well, ha, we’re there." And it wrote itself. Once you’ve got the great idea, they do tend to write themselves, providing you know how to write songs.

So I picked it up at the second verse, it’s a story. It’s him trying to pull a bird, it was about an affair. John told Playboy that he hadn’t the faintest idea where the title came from but I do. Peter Asher had his room done out in wood, a lot of people were decorating their places in wood. Norwegian wood. It was pine really, cheap pine. But it’s not as good a title, "Cheap Pine", baby…

So she makes him sleep in the bath and then finally in the last verse I had this idea to set the Norwegian wood on fire as revenge, so we did it very tongue in cheek. She led him on, then said, "You’d better sleep in the bath". In our world the guy had to have some sort of revenge. It could have meant I lit a fire to keep myself warm, and wasn’t the decor of her house wonderful? But it didn’t, it meant I burned the fucking place down, and then we left it there and went into the instrumental.
— Paul McCartney, <em>Many Years from Now</em>, 1997

I went and bought a sitar from a little shop at the top of Oxford Street called Indiacraft — it stocked little carvings and incense. It was a real crummy-quality one, actually, but I bought it and mucked about with it a bit.<br><br>
Anyway, we were at the point where we'd recorded the ‘Norwegian Wood’ backing track, and it needed something. We would usually start looking through the cupboard to see if we could come up with something, a new sound, and I picked the sitar up — it was just lying around; I hadn't really figured out what to do with it.<br>
It was quite spontaneous: I found the notes that played the lick. It fitted and it worked. 
— George Harrison, <em>The Beatles Anthology</em>, 2000
