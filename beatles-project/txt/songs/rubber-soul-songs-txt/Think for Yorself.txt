Song Facts

• Written and sung by George, “Think for Yourself” criticises dishonesty and encourages independent thinking. Although its lyrics have been interpreted as addressing a romantic partner, Harrison later suggested the song may have been inspired by the government. It followed John’s “Nowhere Man” as one of the first Beatles songs to move away from straightforward romantic themes.

• The track features two bass guitar parts played by Paul McCartney: a standard bass line and a fuzz bass part that serves as the lead guitar throughout the song. It is widely credited as the first recording to use a fuzzbox on a bass guitar, rather than producing distortion accidentally through recording equipment.

• The song has been compared to Bob Dylan’s “Positively 4th Street”, with both songs addressing someone the singer feels has wronged them. Harrison’s use of abstract, Dylan-esque lyrics also reflects Dylan’s influence on the Beatles’ songwriting during this period.

• A short a cappella excerpt of the song appeared in the Beatles’ 1968 animated film <em>Yellow Submarine</em>. Although omitted from the original soundtrack album, it was later included on <em>Yellow Submarine Songtrack</em> in 1999.

Words from George Harrison

Paul used a fuzz box on the bass on ‘Think For Yourself’. When Phil Spector was making ‘Zip-A-Dee-Doo-Dah’, the engineer who’d set up the track overloaded the microphone on the guitar player and it became very distorted. Phil Spector said ‘Leave it like that, it’s great’. Some years later everyone started to try to copy that sound and so they invented the fuzz box. We had one and tried the bass through it and it sounded really good.

— George Harrison, <em>The Beatles Anthology</em>, 2000
