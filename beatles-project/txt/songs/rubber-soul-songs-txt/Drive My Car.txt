Trivia

• "Drive My Car" is primarily written by Paul McCartney, with lyrical contributions from John Lennon.


• The song was omitted from <em>Rubber Soul</em> in North American and replaced by “I’ve Just Seen a Face” as Capitol reshaped the album into more of a folk-rock record to suit American radio trends at the time. It was not released in the US until June 1966, when it appeared as the opening track on the US compilation album <em>Yesterday and Today</em>.

• George Harrison stated that he came up with the song's baseline melody, which was inspired by the baseline from "Respect" by Otis Redding.


Words from Paul McCartney

One of the stickiest [writing sessions in 1965] was "Drive My Car," because we couldn't get past one phrase that we had, "You can buy me golden rings." We struggled for hours; I think we struggled too long. Then we had a break and suddenly it came: "Wait a minute. Drive my car!" Then we got into the fun of that scenario: "Oh, you can drive my car. What is it? What's he doing? Is he offering a job as a chauffeur, or what? And then it became much more ambiguous, which we liked, instead of golden rings which was a bit poofy. "Golden rings' became 'beep beep, yeah'.<br><br>
[Me and John] both came up with that. Suddenly we were in LA: cars, chauffeurs, open-top Cadillacs, and it was a whole other thing. — Paul McCartney, <em>The Beatles Anthology</em>, 2000
