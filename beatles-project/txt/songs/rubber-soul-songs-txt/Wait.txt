Song Facts

• “Wait” is a Lennon–McCartney song written entirely by Paul, who recalls composing it while the Beatles were in the Bahamas filming their second film, <em>Help!</em>.


• After filming <em>Help!</em>, the group were given three days, from 15–17 June, to record enough songs for the second side of the film’s soundtrack album, which was to consist of seven songs not featured in the film. “Wait” was the last of five new songs recorded, but was subsequently shelved after being left unfinished when the group ended the final session half an hour early. This may have been because they already had enough material: alongside the four other new songs recorded during those sessions, they also had “You Like Me Too Much” and “Tell Me What You See”, recorded before filming began but not used in the film, and “Dizzy Miss Lizzy”, which had recently been recorded for the American compilation <em>Beatles IV</em>.

• On the final day of recording , five months after “Wait” had been shelved during the  sessions, the group returned to the unfinished recording, needing a fourteenth track for the album. John and Paul double-tracked their shared vocals, while Paul also double-tracked his solo vocal. John added further rhythm guitar, Ringo added maracas and tambourine, and George played further guitar using a volume pedal.

Words from Paul and George

[Brandon de Wilde, the American actor who accompanied the Beatles to the Bahamas] was a nice guy who was fascinated by what we did. A sort of Brat Pack actor. We chatted endlessly, and I seem to remember writing 'Wait' in front of him, and him being interested to see it being written.
I think it was my song. I don't remember John collaborating too much on it, although he could have.

— Paul McCartney, <em>Many Years from Now</em>, 1997

There was a guy in Liverpool [Colin Manley] who used to go to school with Paul and I, and he was in a band called The Remo Four and played with Billy J. Kramer. [...] He had a volume pedal, and I think we tried that, but I could never coordinate it. So some of those, what we'd do is, I played the part, and John would kneel down in front of me and turn my guitar's volume control.

— George Harrison, <em>Guitar Player</em>, 1987
