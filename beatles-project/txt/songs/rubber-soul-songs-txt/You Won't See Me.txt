Song Facts

• Written by Paul, “You Won't See Me” is inspired by a difficult period in his relationship with then-girlfriend Jane Asher, who had temporarily moved to Bristol to perform in an adaptation of <em>Great Expectations</em> at the Old Vic theatre. The lyrics reflect Paul’s frustration and vulnerability at being unable to contact her during this time.

• The Beatles recorded the song in two takes during the final recording session for <em>Rubber Soul</em>, as they worked to meet the deadline for the album’s pre-Christmas release.

• Running at 3 minutes 22 seconds, it was the longest song the Beatles had recorded up to that point.

• The mono mix features a slightly longer fadeout and runtime.

• Beatles roadie Mal Evans is credited with playing Hammond organ on the track. His single-note contribution can be heard most clearly around the three-minute mark.

• In 1974, pop singer Anne Murray released the song as a single from her ninth studio album, <em>Love Song</em>, reaching No. 8 on the Billboard Hot 100. Anne proudly recalled a compliment from John about her version: “He told me that my cover of ‘You Won’t See Me’ was the best cover of a Beatles song he had heard to that date”.

Words from Paul McCartney

Normally I write on a guitar and have full chords, or on the piano and have full chords, but this was written around two little notes [...] It was 100 percent me as I recall, but I am always quite happy to give John a credit because there’s always a chance that on the session he might have said, ‘That’d be better’.

To me it was very Motown-flavoured. It’s got a James Jameson feel. He was the Motown bass player, he was fabulous, the guy who did all those great melodic bass lines. It was him, me, and Brian Wilson who were doing melodic bass lines at that time, all from completely different angles, LA, Detroit and London, all picking up on what each other did.

— Paul McCartney, <em>Many Years from Now</em>, 1997
