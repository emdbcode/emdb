Song Facts

• “Run for Your Life”, though credited to Lennon–McCartney, was primarily written by John.

• John described the song as a throwaway composition that he never thought much of, despite it being a favourite of George’s.

• In a 1980 interview with <em>Playboy</em>, John recalled that the song borrowed a line from Elvis Presley’s “Baby, Let’s Play House”, which had first been sung by Arthur Gunter in the original version, released a year before Presley’s cover. John had performed “Baby, Let’s Play House” with the Quarrymen on 6 July 1957, the day he first met Paul at the St Peter’s Church garden fête in Woolton, Liverpool.

• “Run for Your Life” remained the first song recorded for the album for most of the recording sessions, until the group decided to include “Wait” on the final day. Most of “Wait” had been recorded several months earlier during the <em>Help!</em> sessions.

• “Run for Your Life” was released as a single exclusively in Italy on Valentine’s Day 1966, backed by “Michelle”.

Words from John and Paul

I never liked ‘Run For Your Life’, because it was a song I just knocked off. It was inspired from — this is a very vague connection — from ‘Baby, Let's Play House’. There was a line on it - I used to like specific lines from songs — ‘I'd rather see you dead, little girl, than to be with another man’ – so I wrote it around that but I didn't think it was that important.
— John Lennon, <em>Rolling Stone</em>, 1970

John was always on the run, running for his life. He was married; whereas none of my songs would have ‘catch you with another man‘. It was never a concern of mine, at all, because I had a girlfriend and I would go with other girls, it was a perfectly open relationship so I wasn't as worried about that as John was. A bit of a macho song. It was largely John's.
— Paul McCartney, <em>Many Years from Now</em>, 1997
