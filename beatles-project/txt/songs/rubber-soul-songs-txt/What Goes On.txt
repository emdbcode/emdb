Trivia

• John and Paul recorded a demo of “What Goes On” in 1963, revealing that the original verse lyrics and melody were replaced in 1965, while the chorus was retained.

• The song was first considered for recording on 5 March 1963, during a session for the group’s third single, “From Me to You” / “Thank You Girl”. The group had hoped to record both “What Goes On” and “One After 909”, but with time for only one more song, they chose the latter, leaving “What Goes On” unused until it was revisited for <em>Rubber Soul</em> in 1965.

• Neil Aspinall, the Beatles’ road manager and assistant, recalled in an April 1966 edition of <em>Beatles Monthly</em> that Paul made a multi-track home demo of “What Goes On” to show Ringo how the song went. Paul performed the vocals and played lead guitar, bass and drums on the tape, after which Ringo listened to it and added his own ideas before the recording session. Ringo later downplayed his contribution at a 1966 press conference, saying, “I contributed about five words to ‘What Goes On’ and I haven’t done a thing since.” Nevertheless, the song earned Ringo his first songwriting credit on a Beatles recording.

• The song was omitted from <em>Rubber Soul</em> in North American and replaced by “It’s Only Love” as Capitol reshaped the album into more of a folk-rock record to suit American radio trends at the time. It was not released in the US until February 1966, when it appeared as the B-side of “Nowhere Man”, another track omitted from the American album. To hear the song in stereo, American listeners had to wait until June, when it was included on the US compilation album <em>Yesterday and Today</em>.

Words from John

That was an early Lennon, written before The Beatles, when we were The Quarrymen, or something like that. And resurrected with a middle eight thrown in, probably with Paul’s help, to give Ringo Starr a song and also to use the bits, because I never like to waste anything.
John Lennon (1980), <em>The Playboy Interviews</em>, 1981
