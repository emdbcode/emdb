Song Facts

• “I’m Looking Through You” was written by Paul about his then-girlfriend Jane Asher, making it a companion piece to the earlier track on the album “You Won’t See Me”.

• The group spent at least 18 hours working on the song across four sessions, held on 24 October and 6, 10 and 11 November. The version heard on the finished recording was assembled during the album’s final two sessions.

• Barry Tashian of The Remains recalled that, while touring with the Beatles on their final US tour in August 1966, he asked Ringo about the unusual percussion sound on “You Won’t See Me”. Ringo told him he was “privy to a great secret” before revealing that he had “just tapped on a pack of matches with his finger”.

• The tape copy of the stereo mix sent to Capitol Records for the US version of <em>Rubber Soul</em> included two false starts by Paul on acoustic guitar. Assuming they were intended to be part of the recording, Capitol’s team left them in, giving the US version a unique opening.

• In 1966, the song was released exclusively in Portugal as the title track of an EP, alongside three other tracks from <em>Rubber Soul</em>.

Words from Paul McCartney

As is one’s wont in relationships, you will from time to time argue or not see eye to eye on things, and a couple of the songs around this period were that kind of thing.

This one I remember particularly as me being disillusioned over her commitment. She went down to the Bristol Old Vic quite a lot around this time. Suffice to say that this one was probably related to that romantic episode and I was seeing through her façade. And realising that it wasn’t quite all that it seemed. 

I would write it out in a song and then I’ve got rid of the emotion. I don’t hold grudges so that gets rid of that little bit of emotional baggage. I remember specifically this one being about that, getting rid of some emotional baggage.

— Paul McCartney, <em>Many Years from Now</em>, 1997
