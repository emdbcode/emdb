Song Facts

• “12-Bar Original” was an instrumental track by the Beatles, their first since signing with EMI in 1962.

• The song was recorded during the <em>Rubber Soul</em> sessions, on the same day the group recorded “What Goes On”. It is unclear whether “12-Bar Original” was intended for the album. However, it was not mixed until the album was being pressed, making it unlikely to have been a serious contender.

• Two takes were attempted, the first breaking down due to a false start by John.

• Norman Smith mixed the track in mono in 1965 for the group’s personal collection. The version released on <em>Anthology 2</em> in 1996 was remixed in stereo by Geoff Emerick and edited down to under three minutes. The unissued original runs for approximately six and a half minutes.

• The full, raw stereo version, along with take 1 and the pre-take jam, are also available to hear, although none have been officially released.

• “12-Bar Original” is one of the few Beatles songs credited to all four members. Others include “Flying”, “Dig It”, “Christmas Time (Is Here Again)”, “Free as a Bird” and “Now and Then”.

• John and Ringo were the only Beatles to speak publicly about the track. Asked in a US radio interview whether there were any unreleased Beatles recordings, John recalled “some lousy 12-bar”. Ringo later stated: “We all wrote the track and I have an acetate of one of the versions.“

• Many fans expressed disappointment that the track was not included on <em>Rubber Soul: Special Edition</em> (2026). Giles Martin, who curated the collection, addressed its omission, explaining: “Some people will say, why didn’t you put the ’12-Bar Original’ on it?’ But well, it’s not very good in the first place, and it’s on the Anthology. And it’s not really linked to the album they were making at the same time.”
